# Talk track and study plan — knowing the business well enough to sell it

Written 2026-10-28 for Daniel, who asked for a plan to get good at talking about Steadfast Code
with clients and anyone else who asks. Two parts: the words (what to say, in order, with the
reasons), and the practice (how to get fluent in four weeks of short reps). Everything here is
true of the business as built; when the prices or the stack change, change this file too.

## Part 1 — the words

### The ten-second answer

"I build websites for small businesses, churches and nonprofits around Lancaster County. They're
custom, they're fast, and the prices are posted on my site. Most are live in two or three weeks."

That is the whole pitch. Everything below is what you say when someone asks a follow-up.

### The one-minute version

"Most small-business websites are a slow template on a hosting plan the owner doesn't understand,
with a monthly fee to keep something they thought they owned. I do it differently. I write custom
code, I use AI tools to do it fast and I'm upfront about that, and I design, check and test every
page myself. The site loads in well under a second, which Google rewards and visitors notice. You
own it outright: your domain, your code, no lock-in. And my prices are on the website, because I
hate it when I can't find a price. A one-page site is $1,500, a full business site is $3,900, and
I can tell you which one you need after a 30-minute call."

### The five things to be able to explain plainly

1. **What a website actually is, for this audience.** A set of pages served to a browser. A
   "static" site is finished pages sitting on a fast network, nothing running behind them. A
   WordPress site is a program that builds each page when someone asks, with a database and plugins
   that need updating. Static is faster, cheaper to host, and has nothing to hack. That is why I
   build static unless there's a real reason not to.
2. **Why speed matters.** The median website is about 2.3 MB. Mine are under 100 KB. People leave
   slow pages, especially on phones; Google ranks speed; and a fast site feels trustworthy before
   anyone reads a word. The home page measures itself live so you can show it, not claim it.
3. **Where it lives and what it costs to run.** Cloudflare's network, which is free for sites like
   these. That's why the care plans are priced on my time, not on hosting, and why a client can
   decline them and still have a working site. Forms go to email through a small function; nothing
   runs on a server I have to patch.
4. **How I build fast, honestly.** AI coding tools write most of the code. I decide what the site
   is, what it says, how it looks, and I read, test on real phones and ship every page. The thing
   that actually sets the schedule is getting the words and photos from the client, so I help with
   those from day one.
5. **What SEO and GEO are, in English.** SEO: making sure people searching for what you do find
   you, mostly on the map. It's a tidy Google Business Profile, consistent facts about you
   everywhere, and real content about real work. GEO: the same, aimed at AI assistants like ChatGPT
   and Google's AI answers, measured by asking them. Nobody honest guarantees rankings; I promise
   the work and a monthly report in plain English.

### Questions you will get, and the answers

- **"Why not just use Wix or Squarespace?"** You can, and for a hobby that's fine. You'll pay
  $20–$50 a month forever, the site will be slower than it should be, and you'll be doing the design
  yourself. I'm for people who want it done well and would rather spend their evenings on their
  business.
- **"My nephew can do it cheaper."** Probably. The question is whether it's fast, whether it shows
  up on Google, whether the forms reach you, and who fixes it in a year. I post my prices so you
  can compare honestly.
- **"Do I need WordPress?"** Only if you need something WordPress does that nothing else does,
  which is rarer than people think. If you do, I'll build it on WordPress and host it properly.
- **"Who owns it?"** You do, the day it's paid. Domain in your name, code in a repository I hand
  over, and I'll help you move if you ever want to.
- **"Can you get me to the top of Google?"** No one can promise that. I can promise the technical
  side is right, the content is real, and you'll see every month exactly what changed.
- **"Isn't it all just AI then?"** The code, mostly. The judgment, the words, the testing, and the
  phone call when something's wrong: me. I'd rather tell you that than have you find out.
- **"How long will it take?"** A one-pager in about a week, a business site in two or three weeks,
  once I have your content. That's usually the slow part, and I'll help with it.
- **"What happens if you get hit by a bus?"** The site keeps running; it needs no one. Everything is
  in a GitHub repository under your name, and any developer can pick it up.
- **"What about sales tax?"** If Pennsylvania requires it for your project, it's added on top and it's
  on the quote, never a surprise on the invoice. (Confirm the rule with the CPA; see the plan.)

### Words to avoid

Hand-coded, handcrafted, artisan (not true). Guaranteed, world-class, cutting-edge (hype). "Cheap"
(say "fairly priced" or just say the price). Any claim about rankings. Any number you haven't
checked.

## Part 2 — the practice

Fluency comes from saying it out loud, not reading it. Fifteen minutes a day, four weeks.

**Week 1 — the pitch.** Each day, say the ten-second and the one-minute answers out loud twice,
once to a mirror and once into the phone's voice recorder. Listen back once. By Friday, say them
without looking.

**Week 2 — the five explanations.** One per day. Explain it to someone who isn't technical (your
wife, a friend, your daughter), then say it again shorter. The test is a nod, not a "huh?"

**Week 3 — questions.** Each day, pick two from the list, have someone ask them cold, answer
without notes. Add any question you got stuck on to the list above, with the answer.

**Week 4 — mock calls.** Three 30-minute discovery calls, with me playing the client over the
bridge or Signal: a plumber with no site, a church secretary with a 2012 WordPress site, a
boutique owner who wants a store. Afterward, I'll write down what landed and what didn't, and we
fix the words.

**Ongoing.** After every real conversation, one line in a notes file: what they asked that you
didn't have a clean answer for. That line becomes the next entry in the question list.
